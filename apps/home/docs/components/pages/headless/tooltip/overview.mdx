# Tooltip

`Tooltip` shows short floating content for its trigger, following the
[WAI-ARIA Tooltip pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/tooltip/).
It handles when to show and hide it, works out where it fits on screen, and
links it to the trigger for assistive technology, so you only bring the look.
For a ready-styled tooltip, use [`@wondesign/tooltip`](/components/tooltip)
instead.

Use it for a short hint about its trigger, like the name of an icon-only
button, a keyboard shortcut, or why a button is disabled. Keep it to plain
text: **links and buttons inside a tooltip can't be reached**.

---

## Key Features

- ✅ **Accessible by Default**: The tooltip describes its trigger for assistive technology
- ✅ **Shows On**: Hover, focus or long press
- ✅ **Hides On**: Pointer leave, blur, <Keyboard>Esc</Keyboard> or a click outside
- ✅ **Auto Flip & Shift**: Flips to the opposite side when space runs short, and shifts to stay inside the viewport
- ✅ **Show & Hide Delays**: Tune how quickly it reacts to the pointer
- ✅ **Controlled & Uncontrolled**: Let it manage the open state, or drive it yourself
- ✅ **Disabled Triggers**: A disabled trigger can still explain itself with a tooltip
- ✅ **Keep Content Mounted**: Hide closed content instead of removing it
- ✅ **Composable**: Render your own elements with [`asChild`](/components/headless/as-child)

## Structure

For what you can configure, refer to the
[Usage tab](/components/headless/tooltip/usage). For every prop, refer to the
[API tab](/components/headless/tooltip/api).

- 🧩 **Tooltip**: coordinates every part. It holds the open state, handles the
  delays and dismissal, and works out the position. **Renders no element of its
  own.**
- 🧩 **Tooltip.Trigger**: the button the tooltip describes.
- 🧩 **Tooltip.Content**: the floating body of the tooltip.
- 🧩 **Tooltip.Arrow**: an optional arrow pointing at the trigger.

```tsx
<Tooltip>
  <Tooltip.Trigger>Save</Tooltip.Trigger>
  <Tooltip.Content>
    Save your changes
    <Tooltip.Arrow>…</Tooltip.Arrow>
  </Tooltip.Content>
</Tooltip>;

// or, with named exports
<TooltipProvider>
  <TooltipTrigger>Save</TooltipTrigger>
  <TooltipContent>
    Save your changes
    <TooltipArrow>…</TooltipArrow>
  </TooltipContent>
</TooltipProvider>;
```

**The arrow must go inside the content.**

## How It Works

A tooltip has to appear when the user shows interest in its trigger, get out
of the way when they don't, and stay on screen. `Tooltip` builds this in,
following the
[WAI-ARIA Tooltip pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/tooltip/):
it decides when the tooltip shows and hides, where it goes, and what screen
readers announce. You don't need to wire up any of it.

### Showing and Hiding

The tooltip shows on hover, after the show delay, and straight away on focus
or a long press. It hides when the pointer leaves, the trigger loses focus,
the user presses <Keyboard>Esc</Keyboard>, or clicks outside.

A click or tap on the trigger **doesn't open the tooltip**. On touch screens,
long press the trigger instead. Moving the pointer from the trigger onto the
content keeps it open.

Long press and clicks outside are handled by
[Pointer](/foundations/pointer), and <Keyboard>Esc</Keyboard> by
[Key Events](/foundations/key-events).

### Placement

If the preferred side doesn't have room, the tooltip **flips to the opposite
side**, as long as that side has more room. It also shifts along its side to
stay inside the viewport. The position is worked out by
[Position](/foundations/position/floating-position), so it behaves exactly as
described there.

### Screen Readers

The content describes its trigger, so screen readers read it after the
trigger's own name.
